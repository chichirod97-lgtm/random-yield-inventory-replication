Random-yield inventory: partial reproduction and loss-function extension

RICHINA ORTANCIA DOMINIQUE（杜米妮） · Course assignment · English companion version

This project studies Yan, Chen, Fu and Bi, Heuristics and deep reinforcement learning for the inventory problem with an all-or-nothing yield pattern and non-zero leadtimes, Computers & Operations Research 186 (2026), 107305. Original article. It is a partial methodological reproduction on a reduced grid and a separate loss-function extension. It does not reproduce all 432 paper instances. AI assisted coding and reporting; available prompts and corrections are disclosed.

Abstract

We compare fixed-weight GDSH, a state-dependent network using exponential loss, and a network minimizing mean discounted cost, on 24 synthetic configurations with three training seeds each. Final evaluation uses 1,000 paths per configuration at seed 1100000. The mean-loss network reduces mean cost relative to LIR and WNH by 5.115% and 4.704%, respectively, averaging percentage gaps equally over configurations and seeds. Its improvement relative to GDSH is 3.340% after averaging three models within each configuration. However, its cost exceeds WNH more often on individual paths. Lower average cost and lower risk relative to a benchmark are distinct objectives.

1. Research question and inventory model

The system has nonzero delivery lead time, uncertain demand, all-or-nothing deliveries and backorders. Each shipment succeeds with probability p, independently in the simulator; otherwise nothing arrives. State consists of inventory and outstanding orders. Each period receives arrivals, chooses an order, observes demand and incurs purchase, holding and backlog costs. Initial inventory and pipeline are zero. Orders in the last L periods are suppressed because they cannot arrive within the horizon. Cost is discounted by alpha.

The study asks whether the implemented policies improve on LIR/WNH on the selected grid, whether teacher initialization and scaling affect numerical behavior, and how changing the training objective affects average performance and risk.

2. Methods and equations (3)–(5)

Equation (3): target stock. The adopted approximation is S=(L+1)mu+k sqrt((L+1)variance+max(L,1)rho²/(1−rho²)(mu²+variance)), where rho²=(1−p)/p and k is the normal quantile at b/(b+h). It combines protection-period mean demand and safety stock for demand/yield uncertainty. It is a cited approximation, not an exact optimal-stock theorem proved in this study. Its denominator requires p>0.5 in this implementation.

Equation (4): WNH. Enumerate all 2^(L−1) binary outcomes for pending orders. For each outcome o, compute g_o=max(S−I−sum(y_i q_i),0). If k shipments succeed, its probability is p^k(1−p)^(L−1−k). WNH sums probability times g_o. LIR instead subtracts the expected pipeline from the target before applying the positive part, with inflation factor one.

Equation (5): GDSH. Replace scenario probabilities with optimized fixed coefficients: q=sum(w_j g_o), with j=1+sum(2^(i−1)y_i). The local implementation additionally makes GDSH weights nonnegative through softplus. State-dependent DRL generates weights from a network; GDSH's weights are fixed across states. Pointwise convexity in weights for a fixed state does not establish global convexity of multi-period neural training.

The network has three ReLU hidden layers of sizes 8,16,16. Teacher-constrained weights lie in WNH±delta, with delta=.25 at L=2 and .0884 at L=5. The implementation adds a nonnegative order projection. Diagnostic variants are precision64, teacher64, scaled64 and teacher_scaled64. The extension teacher_scaled64_mean changes the objective to mean discounted cost. The exponential objective averages exp(100(C−C_WNH)/C_WNH). The mean-loss objective averages C. Both use the same teacher-initialized, state-scaled float64 network in the final comparison. These changes and constraints limit fidelity to the original paper.

3. Experimental design

The grid is L∈{2,5}, p∈{.55,.70,.90}, distribution∈{normal,gamma}, b/(b+h)∈{.90,.95}. Fixed parameters are T=50, demand mean=10, purchase cost=2, holding cost=1, alpha=.99, normal standard deviation=2 and gamma scale=1.6. Normal demand is clipped at zero. Training seeds are 0,1,2, with 500 fixed training paths, 100 validation paths and 300 epochs. Adam starts at .003, multiplies learning rate by .98 each epoch and floors it at .0001. The last model is used; the best validation state is retained separately where implemented.

Training and validation path seeds are 100000+s and 200000+s in the public trainers. Seeds also affect network initialization. GDSH starts at deterministic teacher weights, so its between-seed variation has a different interpretation. Initial diagnostics used instances 13/21; mean-loss diagnostics used 0/12 and test seed 1000000 before extension to the remaining configurations. The final test uses seed 1100000. The investigation was sequential and exploratory, not wholly preregistered. The historical ID-to-configuration manifest is missing; the new runner introduces explicitly labeled canonical IDs.

Paired intervals first average the three fixed models' path costs within a configuration and use an influence-function approximation with a t critical value for the ratio. Bonferroni adjustment covers 24 configurations separately for each benchmark, not all 96 comparisons jointly. These intervals describe test-path uncertainty conditional on trained models, not uncertainty from future retraining. Configurations share a test seed, so a naive independent-configuration global interval is not used.

Two CPU repeated-training examples at instance 0, seed 0, 300 epochs reproduced costs 2739.955324 for GDSH and 2624.412283 for mean loss. The mean-loss historical state dictionary was not bitwise equal, although trajectory differences were about 1.82e−12. This does not verify all 360 historical training runs or all 216 headline models from scratch. 6. Limits and interpretation

This is a synthetic reduced-grid study, not a complete replication, optimality proof or demonstration on real business data. Added feasibility projection, GDSH weight constraints and float precision/initialization differ across methods. GDSH-to-network differences cannot be attributed solely to architecture. Three seeds and 1,000 paths do not establish universal performance or extreme-tail guarantees. Some risk and historical diagnostic claims can only be traced to the report, not independently recomputed from the public aggregate files.

The public release lacks the original mean-loss trainer, the historical grid-ID manifest and complete model/path archives. The new runner reconstructs the documented protocol and saves new outputs separately. Full execution was not undertaken for submission preparation. See resources, duration caveats and commands and actual AI-use provenance.

7. Conclusion

The selected experiments support lower average cost from the mean-cost extension, while exponential loss limits adverse deviations relative to WNH more effectively in this test. Further work should use fresh questions and fixed protocols, more seeds and explicit risk constraints. Reported improvements apply to this implementation and selected configurations.
